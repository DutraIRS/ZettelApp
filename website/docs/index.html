ZettelApp documentation

ZettelApp is a Zettelkasten for Windows: a network of small notes, each holding one idea, connected by links that say how ideas relate. It suggests links while you write, draws your knowledge as a 2D or 3D graph, turns notes into flashcards and quizzes, and works with Anki, Zotero and AI assistants.

Everything stays on your computer unless you ask for something that needs the network.

The ideas behind it

A Zettelkasten ("slip box") is a note-taking method made famous by the sociologist Niklas Luhmann. Each note is atomic (one idea, in your own words) and has a permanent ID. Value comes from the connections: when you link a new note to existing ones, you are forced to think about how ideas relate, and later you can follow those links to find unexpected combinations.

ZettelApp keeps that discipline but removes the friction:

  • IDs are automatic: every note gets a timestamp ID such as 20261027153012.
  • Links can say how notes relate: supports, contradicts, extends, example of, part of, cites, or your own link types.
  • Suggestions surface connections you missed: a classifier compares a note with the rest of your vault and learns from the links you accept and dismiss.
  • Review is built in: notes become flashcards and quizzes, with spaced repetition or Anki.
