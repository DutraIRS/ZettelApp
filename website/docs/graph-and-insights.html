Graph and Insights

The graph

The Graph page (Ctrl+2) draws your notes as a network: each circle is a note, sized by how many links it has, and each line is a link.

Controls

Control What it does
All notes / Local Show the whole collection, or only the open note and the notes within Depth links of it (following links in both directions). Show in graph in a note's ⋯ menu opens the local graph.
2D / 3D Switch between a flat, draggable layout and a 3D view you can orbit.
Colour by Tags, Clusters: k-means, Clusters: hierarchical, or nothing.
k Number of clusters. Auto k picks it for you (see below).
Group Pull notes of the same group together and shade each group's area.
Labels Show note titles. In large graphs, only the best-connected notes are labelled until you hover.
Link types Hide or show individual link types.
Find in graph Type part of a title and press Enter to jump to the note.

At the bottom right are zoom, fit, recompute layout, save as image (PNG) and export these notes (opens the export dialog with the notes on screen).

In 2D, drag a note to move it, drag the background to pan, and use the mouse wheel to zoom. Double-click a note to open it; right-click it for more (open, local graph, quiz on this neighbourhood, export with linked notes). A note's picture is shown inside its circle.

Several links between the same two notes are drawn as separate curves, each with an arrowhead, so none hides another. Hovering or selecting a note dims the other links, keeps the note's own links in their link-type colours, and labels each neighbour with the link types as read from that note, for example Supports · Extended by. This works the same in 2D and 3D.

In 3D, drag to orbit, use the wheel to zoom, and double-click a note to open it. Rotate automatically spins the view. Nearer notes are larger and brighter, so depth is easy to read, and each group appears as a soft coloured halo.

The layout is force-directed (Fruchterman–Reingold): links pull notes together, all notes push each other apart, and gentle gravity keeps separate components on screen. It runs in the background and is cached per view.

Colouring by tag

Each note takes the colour of its most common tag, the one used by the most notes in view, so the big themes of your vault get distinct colours.

Colouring by cluster

Clustering groups notes by meaning, whether or not they are linked. Each note becomes a vector in a semantic space using latent semantic analysis (LSA) of its title, text and tags. Then one of two methods groups the vectors:

  • k-means: Lloyd's algorithm with k-means++ seeding, best of several runs. It is fast and gives round, even-sized groups.
  • Hierarchical: agglomerative clustering with average linkage on cosine distance, computed with the nearest-neighbour-chain algorithm (O(n²)). It works better for uneven, nested topics. Vaults above 4,000 notes fall back to k-means.

With Auto k, ZettelApp tries k from 2 up to about √(n/2) + 2 (at most 12) and keeps the k with the best silhouette score. The score, shown under the legend, measures how well separated the clusters are: 1 is perfect, around 0 means overlapping groups. Each cluster is named by its most distinctive words, the terms more frequent inside it than in the rest of the vault.

Groups and colours

With Group on, notes of the same tag or cluster are pulled towards each other, and each group gets a lightly shaded region with its name. The legend lists the groups with their sizes. Click one to highlight its notes.

Group and chart colours come from a palette validated for colour-vision deficiencies in both light and dark mode. Colours are assigned in a fixed order and never reused: groups beyond the eighth share a neutral grey "Other" colour. Link types use a second cue besides colour, a distinct dash pattern each, and directed types draw arrowheads. The legend always names everything, so nothing depends on colour alone.

Insights

The Insights page (Ctrl+3) summarises your vault:

  • Tiles: notes (and how many are in the Trash), links (and links per note), tags, unlinked notes (good candidates for linking), flashcards, and cards due today.
  • Notes added per month over the last 12 months.
  • Top tags, Links by type and the Most connected notes (click a bar to open the note).
  • Topics: k-means clusters of your notes with their keywords and sizes.

Every chart has a Show as table button (the list icon) with the same numbers as a table, and bars show their value on hover.
